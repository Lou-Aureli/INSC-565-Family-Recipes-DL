## About the Collection

Food is a significant part of every society and culture. At both the individual and national level, food and cuisine serve as crucial sources of identity construction; “in common words, ‘we are what we eat’ and therefore ‘we make ourselves’” (Partarakis et al. 2021). Similarly, since the 1960s, the personal family history or “family archive” has been an increasingly-visible site of the construction of individual and family identity (Woodham et al. 2017). Family recipes serve as a deep connection between these two meaning-making powerhouses–a combination of both tangible and intangible heritage that can mean something different to every person who partakes in it. Our team has made a digital library centered on these recipes in order to allow people living in many different places to share their history, connect with the history of others, and make some tasty food in the process. In its current form, we focus the scope of these recipes on the US South and attempt to capture what is considered “Southern” both in terms of cuisine, culture, and the interaction between them.
